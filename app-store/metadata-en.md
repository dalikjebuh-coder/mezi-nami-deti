## Popis (4000 znaků)

```
Ask “how was school?” and you get “fine.” That's usually where it ends.

The Two of Us is a talking game for parents and kids ages 6 to 9. Your kid draws a question
card and you both answer — them about school and friends, you about work and your own
childhood. You talk out loud; nothing gets written down. It takes a few minutes, and you
still learn things an ordinary day never gets around to.

WHAT'S INSIDE

• Six topics: School, Friends, Fears and worries, Our family, Screens and games, Feelings
• Three depths — from “Warm-up” to “All in.” You decide how far you want to go today.
• 108 questions, each with a gentler version for days when the topic is too big
• Three follow-ups on the back of every card, so the conversation doesn't stop at one sentence
• Bonus cards with challenges and playful questions, to breathe between the serious ones
• Quick Ones — short games for a few minutes in the morning, afternoon or evening

HOW IT WORKS

Pick a topic and a depth. Your kid draws one of three cards. The question is for both of
you — it doesn't matter who starts, though it gets going faster when the parent does.

Before your first talk you agree on three things together: nobody gets in trouble for the
truth, we let each other finish, and saying “stop” is always enough. Without those three
sentences some of these questions would be too much. With them, you can talk about fear,
loneliness, or someone getting hurt.

PRIVACY THAT ISN'T JUST A PROMISE

• No accounts, no sign-up — open it and play
• Answers are never recorded. You talk out loud and the app keeps no trace.
• No analytics, no ads, no tracking
• Works completely offline
• All that stays on your phone is the names you entered and which questions you've done

Optionally, once a week you'll get one question or a small nudge on Sunday evening.
Notifications are scheduled on your phone, with no server involved. You can turn them
off at any time.

WHO IT'S FOR

For parents who want to know more than the grades. The questions are written for kids
between six and nine — around how they think and talk about feelings at that age: open
the subject, name the emotion, close it safely.

One dad built this, not a company. It's free, with no ads and no data collection.

The app is a helper for talking together and does not replace psychological, therapeutic
or medical care. Crisis lines and professional help are listed inside the app.
```
